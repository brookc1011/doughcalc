# Fridge rise for yeasted bread, and when oil goes in (research notes, 2026-11-20)

CJ asked first (2026-11-20) for: mix, fold, shape right away, shaped loaves in the fridge 2-3 days, then bake.

**CJ's decision (2026-11-20), now in the code:** cold bulk, as the sources support. Mix, folds, the tub straight into
the fridge for 2-3 days (48-72 h, default 60 h, the middle), out 30 minutes (BBAH; text names King Arthur's 40-60),
divide and shape, the book's room proof with the finger-dent test, bake. Yeast stays at the book's 0.8 g, labeled
as an assumption. Oil goes in with the salt and yeast after the autolyse; sugar with it is our assumption.

## What the sources say

### Forkish, FWSY (already in `fwsy-bread.md` and `fwsy-general.md`)
- Shaped loaves can go in the fridge right after shaping and bake the next morning, cold, with no warm-up. That is overnight after a full room-temperature bulk, not 2-3 days.
- After the room bulk, dough balls keep in the fridge 2-3 days (Saturday White note, for pizza or focaccia).
- No multi-day fridge rise for a bread loaf, and no yeast amount for one.

### King Arthur, "Can you refrigerate bread dough and bake it later?" (2021)
https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/blog/2021/09/28/how-to-refrigerate-bread-dough-to-bake-later
- Fridge rise "up to about 16 hours, depending on the recipe". Nothing on multiple days.
- Either rise can go in the fridge. For the first rise, about 20-30 minutes on the counter before it goes in.
- After a cold first rise: 40-60 minutes on the counter, slightly puffy, then shape.
- Cold shaped artisan loaves are likely bakeable straight from the fridge. If they haven't puffed at all, give them about an hour on the counter.
- No yeast amount for a fridge version. (Part of this page was read only as a summary.)

### America's Test Kitchen, "Cold fermentation and flavor in yeasted breads"
https://www.americastestkitchen.com/cooksillustrated/how_tos/9169-cold-fermentation-and-flavor-in-yeasted-breads
- Their cold-fermented doughs go "at least 24 to 48 hours and sometimes up to 72 hours".
- No yeast amount, no steps, and doesn't say whether the dough is bulk or shaped.

### Bread Baking at Home, "Retarding the dough" (2023)
https://www.breadbakingathome.com/post/retarding-the-dough-why-what-and-how
- A strong white dough is typically retarded 12-24 hours; some go up to 3 days.
- **Cold bulk:** a few hours of folds, into the fridge right after the last fold. Afterwards, 30 minutes on the worktop to warm up, then divide and shape.
- **Cold proof of shaped loaves:** 15-30 minutes out after shaping, then the fridge. Bake straight from the fridge.
- "Inadvisable to do a second cold proofing." So cold bulk *and* cold proof is not recommended.
- No yeast amount.

### Peter Reinhart, *Artisan Breads Every Day* (2009), Classic French Bread (secondary sources only)
- Method as reported by recipe write-ups: mix, stretch and folds, into the fridge **overnight or up to 4 days**, then out about **2 hours before baking**, shape, proof, bake.
- This is the closest published match for a multi-day fridge rise of a yeasted loaf. The free write-ups do not agree on the yeast amount or type, so **there is no usable yeast figure without the book itself.**

### Oil (and sugar) timing
- Autolyse is flour and water only. Oil goes in after the autolyse, with the salt: The Pantry Mama (oil during the autolyse gets in the way of gluten forming) and the Stadler Made recipe (oil added after the autolyse). Both are blogs, not tested sources.
- Sugar: no source found for bread. Putting it in with the salt and yeast is our assumption.

## What it means for CJ's plan
1. **Folds, shape, then 2-3 days in the fridge (CJ's order): no source supports it.** Every multi-day method found ferments in bulk (Reinhart, Bread Baking at Home, ATK). A fridge proof of shaped loaves is given as overnight to about 16-24 hours (Forkish, King Arthur, BBAH).
2. **The order the sources support for 2-3 days is cold bulk:** mix, folds (about 1½ h), the tub straight into the fridge for 2-3 days, out to warm up (30 minutes per BBAH, 40-60 per King Arthur, about 2 hours before baking per Reinhart), divide and shape, a room proof checked with the finger-dent test, then bake. This also matches what CJ described first ("folds ... 2-3 day bulk ferment, then out to rest and shape").
3. **Yeast:** no source gives a dose for 2-3 days cold. Options: keep the book's 0.8 g and label it as an assumption, or get the figure from Reinhart's book if CJ has it.
4. **Warm-up and proof after the fridge:** the sources range from 30 minutes to about 2 hours. It would go in as an editable setting labeled as an assumption, and the finger-dent test decides.
